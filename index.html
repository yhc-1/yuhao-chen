Yuhao Chen

My research turns the real world into interactive digital twins — virtual counterparts that can be explored, manipulated, and simulated. I work across scales, from small objects to rooms and rinks to entire construction sites, and across the full spectrum of computer vision: 2D and 3D perception, video understanding, 4D reconstruction, and generative modeling. My long-term aim is to make these capabilities accessible enough that anyone can build their own interactive worlds.

Work Experience and Education

I am a Research Assistant Professor at the Vision and Image Processing Lab (VIP) at the University of Waterloo, in the Department of Systems Design Engineering. I joined the VIP lab as a postdoctoral fellow under the supervision of Professor Alexander Wong in 2020, was promoted to Research Associate in 2022, and became a Research Assistant Professor in 2023.

I earned my B.Sc. and Ph.D. degrees in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Purdue University in 2015 and 2019, respectively, where I was a member of the Video and Image Processing (VIPER) laboratory under the supervision of Professor Edward J. Delp. My dissertation was on estimating phenotypic traits from RGB imagery.

I currently co-supervise 5 PhD and 9 MASc students, and have supervised or worked with 130 undergraduate students. I have published 93 papers; my citation record is on Google Scholar.

What I Work On

My program spans four application areas that share one set of underlying problems — dynamic scene understanding, 3D/4D reconstruction, and physics-aware generation:

  • Food. Video-based dietary monitoring and intake estimation, 3D food geometry and portion measurement, and generative models for food. This is my most demanding testbed, since food stretches, tears, mixes, melts, and flows.
  • Sports. Reconstructing players and gameplay from broadcast video, including 3D human mesh recovery, rink registration, action recognition, multi-player tracking, and puck localization.
  • Construction. Long-term, UAV-based monitoring of outdoor construction sites, including large-scale change detection and level-of-detail Gaussian splatting.
  • Robotics. Robotic grasping and bin picking, real-time Gaussian-splatting SLAM, 3D scene graphs, and embodied question answering.
